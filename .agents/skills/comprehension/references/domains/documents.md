# Comprehending documents

Identify edition/revision, author or speaker, genre, date and approved corpus. Use stable section/page/table/paragraph locations and preserve surrounding context for quotations. Distinguish a document's assertion, attributed testimony, cited external work and investigator inference. References do not prove cited contents.

Map substantive regions before claiming broad coverage. Read complete short texts when useful; for larger works identify examined sections and concrete omissions. Preserve how arguments connect premises to conclusions, how narrative attributes events, and how examples, qualifications, counterarguments and scope constrain interpretation. Keep ambiguous wording ambiguous unless authorized evidence resolves it.

For each retained claim, check both source-to-meaning omission and meaning-to-source support. A nearby passage or large quotation does not automatically entail the wording. Distinguish silence in examined text from absence in a larger work or in the world. When edition or OCR/layout damages meaning, name the affected passage and uncertainty; do not silently normalize a doubtful reading.
