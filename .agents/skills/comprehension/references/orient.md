# Orient to material

Orient when structure, source boundaries or coverage are the current gap; it is not a declaration of understanding. Identify source identities/revisions and approved corpus. Inspect structural map and sample content in context: chapters, sections, paragraphs, tables, captions, quotations, notes and references. Parent and neighboring regions can change an isolated passage's meaning. Treat external reference targets as unread until actually inspected within scope.

Identify purpose, genre, attribution and temporal perspective. Note where arguments, mechanisms, narrative, measurements or exceptions occur; retain unexpected regions rather than forcing them into a preconceived theme. A short text may be read whole; for larger material, combine overview with explicit unexamined regions and targeted descent. Do not call sampled reading exhaustive.

Return a usable map with stable navigation, examined/unexamined/unavailable regions, consequential unfamiliar terms and candidate questions. Distinguish structural absence from content not yet read. Note parsing/access gaps. Orientation ends when it enables the next useful inquiry, not when every passage is interpreted.
