# Learn from useful experience

Use when an authorized task, requested reflection or recurring difficulty makes experience relevant. A useful attempt may revise understanding of the world or suggest a better method; this reference is the canonical procedure for procedural learning, not a mandatory review of every task. It applies with project records, research notes or a wiki. Do not create a lesson archive or claim a learned capability from a saved note alone.

## Capture evidence and diagnose

Keep the original requested outcome separate from the agent's plan. When useful, preserve task purpose, the specific resource and version used, relevant conditions, expected result if stated prospectively, observed evidence and uncertainty in the owning project/evidence record. Distinguish requirements, forecasts, assumptions and preferences. Do not manufacture a prediction after the event.

Diagnose the boundary before changing a procedure: world knowledge or source evidence, context/retrieval delivery, interpretation/application, tooling, changed circumstances or outcome reporting. A useful attempt can update world knowledge without changing procedure. A bad result does not by itself identify the cause; retain failed, uncertain and inconclusive outcomes. A no-change conclusion is valid. Keep no-lesson comparisons and relevant negative cases where they help test selection or attribution, without requiring benchmarks.

## Form a bounded candidate

A procedural candidate identifies the actual skill, tool or other resource and revision, observed evidence, diagnosis, recognizable trigger and applicability conditions, exceptions, bounded proposed revision and expected observable benefit. Compare the candidate against the evidence. One incident may justify a local factual correction, not a general rule. Preserve the original method/version where useful and update one canonical resource with brief links from relevant skills rather than duplicating its playbook.

Make a worthwhile candidate findable from the method or project navigation that later matching work will consult. Keep its trigger and unproven status visible without turning it into an active global rule. Do not load every episode or paste the full history into startup context.

Candidate, adoption, edit, activation and outcome are different states. A proposal is not an instruction. Authorized, reversible maintenance may be performed under standing scope without per-edit approval for routine factual fixes; an experience does not authorize unrelated, irreversible or external changes. Record adoption and implementation distinctly. A file edit does not prove installation, host loading or effective activation.

## Check later use

On later relevant work, establish whether the resource was encountered, applied appropriately and helpful. Compare actual evidence and important changed conditions; include a nearby non-applicable case when useful. Preserve non-application, regression, uncertainty and inconclusive results. No fresh comparison means the candidate remains unproven, not that learning succeeded. Narrow, revise or withdraw a procedure when later evidence warrants it. Do not require a benchmark or per-edit human review for ordinary authorized reversible maintenance; assess the strength of broad reusable rules proportionally to their claims.
