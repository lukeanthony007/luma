# Keep purpose and progress connected

Use when returning to an undertaking, choosing a useful focus, reviewing a period of work, handling changed circumstances, or preparing for an interruption or handoff. Continuity preserves why the work matters, where it stands and what remains to be done across conversations, models and time. It should help the person regain their bearings and move forward without repeating their story.

Follow the [working agreement](../../AGENTS.md). [luma-setup](../luma-setup/SKILL.md) helps establish an initial direction; [luma-wiki](../luma-wiki/SKILL.md) holds readable understanding; [luma-memory](../luma-memory/SKILL.md) governs evidence and corrections. Continuity connects that understanding to ongoing purpose and commitments. Keep useful context in the existing wiki or established project records, with one authoritative home per fact. Do not create a separate continuity database, obligatory calendar hierarchy or empty folders.

## Regain the situation

Read the current request and relevant retained context before asking the person to recap. Recover the original purpose separately from the agent's proposed plan. Inspect the current state of relevant files, results or other available evidence when the next action depends on them. A handoff is a guide to verification, not proof that everything it describes is still current.

Build only the picture needed for the next decision: what the person wants, what is established, what remains open, relevant constraints and decisions, who owns any commitments, and what could happen next. Link to details rather than loading or repeating the whole history. Preserve consequential uncertainty and identify missing context instead of filling gaps with an invented narrative.

Offer a brief orientation when it helps: where we left off, what changed and the next useful choice. Ask one focused question if a consequential gap remains. When the request and evidence are already clear, continue within existing authorization without requiring a recap or renewed approval. New messages normally steer the active undertaking; preserve earlier obligations unless the person changes or cancels them. If they choose a different purpose, acknowledge the change and retain the disposition of the old work rather than silently losing it.

## Connect purpose and time

Treat the purpose of work and the period committed to it as separate coordinates. A project can span months; a particular contribution can belong to this session, a day or a week. Finishing a period's review does not finish every project it touched. One period may serve several purposes, and one purpose may receive attention across many periods.

Use only the horizons that help this person: a focus session, an upcoming event, a week or a longer direction. Do not manufacture annual goals, daily logs or a fixed Year/Month/Week/Day tree. Help the person connect a proposed next action to the outcome it serves, and identify an achievable contribution for the relevant period.

Distinguish an idea, a recommendation, an agreed commitment, a deadline, an estimate and a review date. Retain the owner and any dependency or condition when known. Resolve relative dates into an explicit date and time zone when precision matters, asking only if the context cannot establish them. Do not infer a due date, another person's agreement or permission to act from importance alone.

Fit commitments to actual circumstances: available time, existing responsibilities, shared resources and the person's stated capacity. Present meaningful conflicts and options such as narrowing scope, sequencing work, waiting or changing the commitment. Do not invent numerical capacity or rank every life concern with one priority score. Energy and acceptable workload may require the person's judgment. The agent can recommend a focus without scheduling or committing on their behalf beyond its authority.

## Make progress observable

Before a meaningful attempt, preserve enough of the intended outcome to judge what happens. Keep requirements, predictions, assumptions and preferences distinct. A requirement describes what must hold; a prediction describes what is expected; an assumption is provisional; a preference guides a tradeoff. Use ordinary language and only the distinctions relevant to the work, without forcing a measurement plan onto every conversation.

As work proceeds, record material results and changes in understanding with their supporting evidence or attributed user report. Distinguish planned, attempted, observed and verified. A successful tool call, a finished document or a collaborator's return does not alone establish that the original purpose was satisfied. Use [luma-collab](../luma-collab/SKILL.md) for delegated contributions and their integration; retain responsibility for the larger outcome.

When a result challenges the strategy, revisit its assumptions and the user's purpose. Change the plan when justified while preserving what was tried and why. Do not rewrite earlier expectations to match later results. An unsuccessful trial may still answer a useful question; lack of a result remains unresolved rather than becoming evidence of success or failure.

## Review and carry forward

At a useful stopping point, interruption or requested review, compare intended work with what actually happened. Account for completed, partial, blocked, uncertain, deferred and deliberately stopped work as applicable. Do not require a ceremonial review after every exchange or infer inactivity from silence.

For unfinished work, decide within the person's direction whether to continue, revise, wait, transfer or stop. Carryover is a renewed commitment linked to the earlier unfinished one: retain the prior outcome and explain why another period of attention is appropriate. Do not silently move dates, erase missed expectations or automatically treat every open item as tomorrow's obligation. A deadline passing does not establish that an unobserved external action failed.

For waiting work, retain the dependency and the event or review point that would make reconsideration useful. Identify who can provide the missing input when known. A written reminder or review date does not schedule a notification. Use an already authorized scheduler only if one is actually available, and verify its setup before claiming it will run; otherwise explain that the item can be revisited in a later interaction.

Let meaningful outcomes from shorter periods inform longer-term understanding. Explain what they support, what remains uncertain and whether the larger direction still fits. Do not turn a collection of checked tasks into a claim that a broader goal was achieved. Repeated carryover may justify discussing scope, obstacles or a changed priority; avoid assuming laziness or applying pressure to maintain an obsolete goal.

## Preserve a useful handoff

Save consequential context as it develops rather than relying on a final message that may never happen. Before a deliberate handoff, leave a concise current account with the purpose, important decisions and reasons, actual results and evidence, open commitments, relevant authority bounds, blockers and the next useful action or condition. Include exact artifact or operation references when needed to distinguish what was done. This is a relevance guide, not a mandatory template; omit empty fields and avoid transcript dumps.

Keep current context easy to find through wiki navigation. Preserve meaningful prior outcomes with dates or links when they explain a change, and reconcile human edits before updating. Use [luma-inbox](../luma-inbox/SKILL.md) only for an actual question, worksheet or authorized background handoff that needs the person's attention. Durable continuity must survive clearing a handled inbox item.

On resumption, distinguish completed work from uncertain execution. If an external action may have happened but its result is missing, inspect the available operation record or current state before considering another attempt. A timeout or missing note does not establish that nothing happened. Do not replay an uncertain send, purchase, deployment or other consequential action merely to get a clean result. Preserve the uncertainty and seek the evidence or decision needed to proceed. A later successful attempt must not rewrite an earlier failure.

## Portable practice and Animus

This skill provides a method for maintaining context and choosing what follows. It does not create background activity, durable execution identities, transactional recovery, authority enforcement or guaranteed delivery. A harness must actually load the relevant guidance and records for them to inform a session.

When an Animus integration is present, follow its current owning work, knowledge and recovery interfaces. Reconcile wiki edits through that integration rather than inventing accepted records, IDs or a parallel execution ledger. Use real returned references; a Markdown status does not settle runtime work. Keep execution settlement, user-goal satisfaction and delivery distinct.

Assess continuity through the person's ability to regain context, understand what remains open and take an appropriate next step. A saved handoff or completed period review is evidence of recordkeeping; useful continuation must be observed in later work.
